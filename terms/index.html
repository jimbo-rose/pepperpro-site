In one sentence

PepperPro is a calculator and a logbook. It does arithmetic on numbers you type in, it keeps a record of what you tell it, and nothing it shows you is medical advice.

Using the app means you accept these terms. You are asked to confirm them once, on first run, before any dose arithmetic is drawn.

Last updated 19 October 2026.

What the app does

It converts between mcg, mg, mL and syringe marks, works out how much water to add to reach the dose you chose, and counts how many doses a vial holds. It shows the working behind every figure.

It keeps the record: vials, doses, schedules, measurements, lab results, notes and photographs, on one timeline.

Every figure in the app is either one you typed or arithmetic on one you typed. Nothing in it is a number this app decided you should use.

What the app is not

It is not a medical device, and it is not a substitute for a clinician, a pharmacist or a laboratory.

It does not recommend a dose, a schedule, a route or a product, and it never fills one in for you.

It does not tell you whether a dose is right or safe for you, and it does not diagnose, treat or monitor any condition.

It does not check interactions between anything you take, and it raises no warning about combinations. There is no interaction checking anywhere in this app, because that needs a licensed data source and a regulatory posture this app does not have.

It cannot verify what is in your vial. The contents, the source, the strength and the purity of anything you record are things the app knows only because you typed them.

It derives no expiry. The app records dates you give it and will not estimate a beyond-use date or how long a mixed vial keeps.

What is yours to check

Check every calculation before you draw or inject anything. Read what the screen says against the strength printed on the vial and the scale printed on the barrel of the syringe in your hand.

A mark on a U-100 syringe and a mark on a U-40 syringe are different amounts of liquid — two and a half times apart, on barrels that look the same. The app counts marks on the scale you told it you were using and has no way to see the one you are holding.

Whether what you record is legal where you live is yours to know. Compounds described in the reference library are approved medicines in one country and not approved for human use in another.

PepperPro is for adults. It is not directed at children and it is not intended for anyone under 18.

Talk to a qualified healthcare professional before making any decision about a substance you take.

The library, the prices and the shop

The reference library is educational. An entry describes what a compound is and what it has been studied for; it holds no dose, schedule or amount, it is not a recommendation to take anything, and being listed in it is not an endorsement.

A regulatory status quoted in an entry is a restatement of what a regulator published. It is not legal advice, and published positions change.

A lab report on file means a document exists and names a lab. It is never a claim that the vial in your hand contains what that document describes.

Prices are what a seller's own page said when it was last checked. They are not quotes, nothing is held for you, and PepperPro is never a party to a sale of a substance — no cart, no stock, no fulfilment.

Anything that earns us a commission says so on the same screen, before you use it. Commission never changes what is listed, what comes first, or what is already ticked.

Your records

Your records are yours. They are kept on your device, you can export the lot at any time, and Settings ▸ Erase removes every one of them.

There is no server holding a copy, which means there is no copy for anyone to restore. A device that is lost, wiped or replaced takes the records with it unless you had a backup or an export.

What the app does with your records, what it reads from Apple Health, and the features that can send something out are all set out in the privacy policy.
