Type what is printed on the vial and the amount you intend to draw each time. The page answers with how much water to add so that amount lands on a mark you can read, and shows every step of the arithmetic.

The vial

As printed on the label. Powder only — the water is the answer, not a question.

Most 10 mg vials hold 3 mL. The answer never asks for more water than the glass can take.

Each dose

Your number. This page does not suggest one. 1 mg is 1,000 mcg.

The syringe

Printed on the barrel. "Draw 10 units" is 0.10 mL on U-100 and 0.25 mL on U-40 — two and a half times the liquid for the same number. Check it every time.

"30-unit", "50-unit" and "100-unit" insulin syringes are 0.3, 0.5 and 1 mL. A 3 or 5 mL syringe is marked in millilitres, not units.

How it was worked out

    Concentration is mass over volume. Volume per dose is dose over concentration. Marks are volume times marks-per-millilitre for the scale you chose. Doses in the vial is vial over dose, and no amount of water changes it.

    Fill in the vial and the dose and the answer appears here.

    Keep it on your phone

    In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens like an app and remembers the last numbers you typed. Nothing you type leaves this page.

    This page does arithmetic on the numbers you type. It recommends nothing, cannot verify what is in any vial, and is not medical advice. A draw of fewer than five marks is hard to read on any barrel; the page says so when that is the case.
